## How it works

The tilt-shift look of the genre: a narrow, pitched-down camera and a lens so shallow that only a band of the street around the party is sharp, with the foreground and the distance melting away, so a full-size town reads as a model on a table. This effect recreates it two ways and picks a default and a fallback.

The scene is the village (`src/effects/diorama`, staged by `src/gpu/diorama/stage.ts`): terraces, stairs, characters, two lamps whose glass the scene file makes glow at five times lit white (`"glow"` in `assets/scenes/village.json`), drawn at 4× MSAA with alpha-to-coverage into an HDR target and a `depth24plus` buffer from plan.md's camera (pitch 35°, field of view 25°). The focus is set relative to the camera's focus point, 2.5 units in front of it by default, so the party on the front of the plaza is sharp and the back terrace and the shore soften. Then the lens, then bloom and output: plan.md's order, depth of field before bloom.

The lens is `src/gpu/lens/tilt-shift.ts` (`createTiltShift`), reusable by any effect that has an HDR colour target:

- **`depth`** (the default) is a half-resolution gather after Jimenez (2014), ported from Moon Temple's `src/gpu/dof/gather.wgsl`. The circle of confusion comes from the depth buffer, linearised with the projection's third and fourth rows (perspective, reversed or orthographic alike). WebGPU cannot resolve depth, so a 4× buffer is read with `textureLoad` on `texture_depth_multisampled_2d`, sample 0; a single-sampled one with `texture_depth_2d` (the `msaa` control switches between them). The focus is a band rather than a thin lens: sharp within `focus band / 2` of the focus distance, growing linearly to the largest blur over a near and a far transition, the focal region and transition regions a game lens exposes. In this scene the visible ground runs from about 5 units in front of the focus to about 18 behind it, so one symmetric slope would blur one side far more than the other. Six passes (`tilt-shift.wgsl`): a prefilter that averages 2 × 2 blocks and gives each the circle of its nearest pixel; a spread pass that smears each texel over the cell one tap of its kernel stands for; the largest near circle per 16-pixel tile, dilated over the tiles it can reach; a 49-tap ring gather into separate far and near fields; and a full-resolution composite. A far tap counts only where its own circle reaches back to the pixel, and taps in front of focus are left to the near field, so a blurred background never bleeds onto a sharp character; the near field looks as far as the nearest blurred foreground can reach, so a blurred near tree spreads over the sharp street behind it with soft, partial coverage.
- **`screen`** needs no depth. The circle grows with distance from a horizontal line, the tilt-shift photo filter: a 2 × 2 downsample, a horizontal and a vertical Gaussian at half resolution (sigma half the radius, at most 12 taps a side), and a composite. The radius depends on the row alone, so the horizontal pass is exact.

Both write the same inspector views: the **circle of confusion** (the scene in grey, warm where the foreground is blurred, cool where the background is), the **near field** and the **far field** (for `screen`, the blurred image below and above the line). The stages are the lens, the bloom and the lamps' glow.

**Which is the default.** The study ran before the diorama existed, on a deeper street of sprites that it keeps as its fixture (`experiments/tilt-shift-street.ts`: about 5 units of ground in front of the focus and 18 behind, the harder case). `experiments/tilt-shift-study.test.ts` uses the depth lens's own prefilter as a depth probe at 960 × 540, then compares, for every half-resolution block, the depth method's circle with the screen method's (`experiments/tilt-shift-results.json`). With no camera roll the ground's view distance is a function of the row alone, so a screen lens fitted to the ground matches it almost exactly (mean error 0.1 px). Everything standing up is a different matter: with the setting that fits the whole picture best, 75% of upright blocks (characters, lanterns, trees) are wrong by more than 2 pixels, 69% of in-focus uprights are blurred and 49% of blurred ones are left sharp. In pictures: the screen lens blurs the tops of the characters standing in focus and keeps the near knight and the near-corner tree sharp where they cross the band; the depth lens blurs each by its distance. Sprites are the subject of the genre, so **the depth method is the default** (Tier B, provisional). **The screen method is the Tier A fallback**: it costs about a fifth as much, it needs no depth buffer, so it also works in compatibility mode (which cannot `textureLoad` a depth texture, docs/support-matrix.md), on flat layers such as 9.4's parallax header, and on images, and it is what adaptive quality (12.3) drops to first.

## Cost

Measured on an Apple M1 Max on Dawn (`npm run study -- --epic 5`, [docs/studies/epic-5.md](../../../docs/studies/epic-5.md)), marginal cost per frame of the whole effect (village, lens, bloom and output): **1.6 to 1.7 ms at 1920 × 1080** with the depth lens, against 0.9 to 1.1 ms with the lens off, so the depth lens costs about 0.6 to 0.8 ms; the screen lens costs 0 to 0.3 ms (0.9 to 1.3 ms in all). At 1266 × 585 the depth lens's effect is 1.6 ms, the screen lens's 0.8 and the scene alone 0.5. The depth lens's runs vary more than anything else measured (1.0 to 3.4 ms across rounds), and the blur size makes no difference inside that noise. Earlier, with other agents on the GPU, `experiments/tilt-shift-study.test.ts` timed the lens alone at 1.0 to 1.6 ms (depth) and 0.2 ms (screen) on its street. Nothing has been measured on an integrated GPU, where the A and B budgets are set. The effect's own chunk is 8.9 kB gzipped, 57.3 kB with what it shares.

Memory beyond the scene: the depth lens keeps four half-resolution `rgba16float` targets (16.6 MB at 1080p) and two tile-sized `r16float` ones; the screen lens three (12.4 MB); each method's targets are allocated only once it is used. The effect also needs a full-resolution sharp copy of the scene (16.6 MB), because the lens cannot read and write the same target. Per output pixel the depth lens makes about 60 texture reads (most in the gather's 2 × 49 taps at half resolution), the screen lens about 15. The circle is capped at 48 pixels, the gather's ceiling, and kept above 4 so the lens still shows on a thumbnail.

## Pitfalls

- **Upright sprites are vertical cards.** Seen from 35° above, a sprite four units tall spans about 2.3 units of view depth, head nearer than feet. A focus band thinner than that blurs a character's head while its feet stay sharp, which looks like a smear rather than a lens. The default band is 4 units deep, wider than a character's span, centred 2.5 units in front of the camera's focus point, where the party stands. A batcher that wrote each sprite's feet depth over the whole quad would remove it; that is not done.
- **The screen lens is right only for the ground.** It cannot tell a tall foreground object from the street at the same height in the frame, so anything crossing the band keeps or loses its blur by row, not by distance. Its controls default near the fit to this scene's ground, nudged up to keep the subject's head in the band.
- **What a blurred foreground hides is not in the image.** The far field under a near object is a guess from the background just outside it; without that guess a blurred near tree turns into an opaque slab (Moon Temple's layered method hit the same thing).
- **Small bright lights.** At large blur a lantern drew the gather's rings until the spread prefilter and the per-pixel jitter of the ring radii were ported from Moon Temple. Lights become soft shapes of the glass, not discs. Shaped bokeh is story 5.2 (`bokeh-highlights`, `src/gpu/lens/bokeh.ts`): with `bokehThreshold` above 0 the lens holds back light above it, and a pass after the lens draws it again as aperture-shaped discs. The screen lens's Gaussian makes soft blobs.
- **MSAA edges.** The circle comes from depth sample 0 while the colour is the resolve of four samples, so a silhouette's edge pixel can take the wrong side's circle. The prefilter takes the nearest of each 2 × 2 block, which errs towards the foreground.
- **Depth precision.** The camera's near plane is 1, not 0.1, so `depth24plus` resolves the far street to well under a tenth of a unit.
- The gather's per-pixel noise is fixed to the screen; with the camera still the picture is stable, but it has not been checked through the loupe under a panning camera.

## When to use it

The depth lens over any diorama scene or hero that draws a depth buffer: it is the look of the genre, provisionally Tier B. The screen lens where there is no depth buffer or no budget: Tier A pages, phones, compatibility mode, flat parallax layers, and as the first step down in adaptive quality. Both take their focus every frame, so a rack focus (5.3) only has to animate `focusDistance` or `line`; `viewDistance` in the lens module gives the focus distance of an object.
